Just a few days ago, Levent Alpöge [proved](https://alpo.ge/remark.pdf) the Riemann Hypothesis with the help of an LLM. The Riemann hypothesis shines some light on the distribution of primes. Primes are famously chaotic in their distribution. To understand the distribution of primes suggests an understanding of computation itself.

There is something magical about systems with emergent complexity, such that simple rules lead to chaotic systems. These systems can be analog, like the three body problem.

![three body problem](https://i.imgur.com/jDZD73p.gif#center)

Or, they can be discrete, like Conway's Game of Life.

![game of life](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/e/e5/Gospers_glider_gun.gif?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=imageinfo&utm_content=thumbnail_unscaled#center)

It is perhaps of no shock then that I've strived to follow directly in Conway's footsteps. I am amazed by his ability to reframe difficult theory to be digestible. Rest in peace Conway, I can only imagine what you would have achieved with the modern tools available to us.

![conway](https://i.ytimg.com/vi/fPVXBurxfU8/mqdefault.jpg#center)

Instead, the solve of the Riemann Hypothesis came with no radical new theory, no brilliant framing. Instead we have a *17 million* lines of Lean proof funded by a PR arm. For as big of an achievement this is for Alpöge, I cannot help but feel that the romantic era of brilliant framings is waning. No longer do we need to communicate across generations, and thus refine theory to be digestible. Instead mathematics is available to the highest bidder, disseminated at will to the masses, with the costs paid in carbon.

I have no doubt that the brilliant framings will come in time. But I worry they will be greatly outpaced by the frontier.
